## Answered decisions

### Q1 — Product identity (accepted direction; wording subject to final sign-off)

Secretary is an open-source, always-available, general-purpose personal AI assistant. It can integrate with a smart home, potentially a car, and future services. Coding-agent orchestration is an initial capability, not the defining product boundary. Users should be able to bring supported AI harnesses/providers/subscriptions/API keys and choose execution infrastructure: personal hardware, VPS, cloud/serverless control plane and cloud execution nodes, potentially isolated with microVMs. Cloudflare is a target deployment option, not an exclusive dependency. Maintain continuous identity and context across integrations and deployment choices.

Unresolved downstream constraints: supported subscription compatibility and provider terms; formal BYO compute portability contract; permission boundaries and privacy/data ownership guarantees; cloud/serverless execution split; edition distinctions; public-v1 vs later scope.

### Q2 — AI compatibility contract (accepted)

Secretary is provider-agnostic through extensible adapters. Supported harnesses, subscriptions, API credentials and local models may be combined or selected by the owner. Compatibility is verified per integration, not promised universally. No dependency on a single provider or model.

### Q3 — Deployment portability (accepted)

Secretary's canonical personal state and identity should be portable across supported self-hosted, VPS and serverless/cloud deployment options; Execution Nodes can independently run on owner hardware, VPS or isolated cloud microVMs. Hybrid topology is supported as a product goal. Platform support is verified per deployment rather than assumed; migrations, backups and native session portability require separate design.

## Q4 — Household identities (exploration, NOT decided)

Owner proposes one persistent Secretary persona/identity shared by a household, with different behavior, memory and configuration for each recognized speaker (e.g. Nick, Ora, Terry, unknown guest). Proposed human-readable layout is `/users/{UserName}/config.toml`, `Memory.md` etc. This is a conceptual layout only: persisted identity and state representation, particularly on Durable Objects/serverless, remain open.

Known conflict with current v1 model: `GLOSSARY.md` defines `Person` as the sole owner of `Personal Conversation`; `spec/spec.md` requires exactly one owner and one ordered Personal Conversation, and restricts Telegram commands to the configured owner. Shared Secretary persona does not imply shared chat histories or shared authorization.

Open branches to settle before glossary or canonical spec changes:
- owner vs household member vs child vs guest; enrollment, removal and account linking
- per-person/private conversations and memory vs explicitly shared household memory, tasks, artifacts and scenes
- voice speaker recognition as fallible attribution vs proof of authorization; unknown/ambiguous speaker behavior and actions requiring verification
- shared smart speakers with multiple people, interrupted interactions and concurrent turns
- cross-user data leakage, guest and child retention/consent policies
- stable immutable Person ID independent of rename; config files as portable exports/projections vs authoritative server state
- migration of current one-owner deployments, initial release vs later product scope

### Q4 exploration — separate personal Secretaries and collective decisions (NOT decided)

The owner refined the model: Nick and Ora each have an independent personal Secretary and see only their own queries when opening their respective Telegram chats. Some questions (movie, meals, holidays, plans, purchases, guests, shared schedules) require multiple people's opinions and cannot be solved by either personal Secretary alone.

Candidate model, pending confirmation:
- `Person`: human identity with private preferences and conversation.
- Personal `Secretary`: independently addresses one Person and preserves their private conversation, state and authorized actions.
- `Circle` (working name): a membership/policy boundary for a group of people (household, friends, etc.), not another personal Secretary and not an AI-controlled superuser.
- `Shared Decision`: a durable scoped request requiring input or consent from specified participants, with explicit lifecycle and outcome.
- `Facilitator` (working name): the Circle's coordination role/workflow; may use LLM for explanation or option synthesis but does not autonomously impersonate participants or confer authorization.

Illustrative flow: Nick asks his Secretary "What film do we want to watch?"; it creates a Circle decision involving Nick and Ora. Each person's Secretary delivers a private request for their opinion. Only responses deliberately submitted to the shared decision are made visible to other participants. The Facilitator proposes overlap, seeks any required agreement, records the outcome in shared Circle history, and sends a personal notification to each participant. A unilateral answer is not falsely presented as consensus. This is a shared coordination domain, not A2A conversation as the source of truth.

Current conflicts: existing `GLOSSARY.md` defines Person as owner and Secretary as tied to one Personal Conversation; `spec/spec.md` requires one configured owner and one Personal Conversation in v1. This exploration implies multiple personal Secretaries plus an additional shared-scoped domain and requires subsequent deliberate changes to canonical terms/spec/release boundaries. No glossary term or ADR is approved yet.

Branches still open: membership and participants; invite/consent policies; definition of agreement (unanimous, quorum, alternatives); what may be read from private memory vs only explicitly submitted opinions; shared events vs private chat projections; authority over tools and shared resources; timeouts and unavailable participants; conflicts and changes of mind; persistence and portability across separate deployments.

### Q4b — Circle lifetime and A2A collaboration (exploration, NOT decided)

Owner proposes a Circle that can be one-off or persistent/reusable depending on the task. Examples:
- Movie/meal decisions with Nick and Ora: repeat frequently; personal Secretaries can share relevant preference summaries and viewing history in a scoped collaborative session.
- 1–2 day activity holiday with X, Y, Z: one person creates a Circle and invites the others; each participant's Secretary gathers constraints (budget, available dates, activity suitability) and participates in proposals/negotiation.

A2A has become a plausible participant-communication mechanism. Shared Circle lifecycle/decisions remain explicit server-owned state, not a prompt passed indefinitely between agents.

Candidate distinctions, pending design agreement:
- Secretary: long-lived personal assistant with private memory and tools.
- Circle: scoped multi-Secretary collaboration session, short-lived by default; can be resumed/reused or created from a reusable membership group.
- Decision: specific question, proposals, participant responses, agreement and outcome within a Circle.
- Optional reusable membership object separate from a single session, if recurring household use merits it.

Critical edge cases:
- Share only a context projection needed for the question, with participant control; do not publish full personal watch histories, personal health records, private calendars or unrelated memory by default.
- A history of lending money is not an offer or authorization to lend $100; Circle may ask Z, not commit them.
- A leg injury must be considered when suggesting activities such as skydiving or skiing; Secretary should not conclude safety from an unverified health summary.
- Date availability and budgets need direct confirmation where uncertain; Circle proposals do not create commitments.
- Circle should be recoverable while active but need not live forever after resolution; decide archive/retention rules separately.
- Separate private Telegram chats remain personal views; shared Circle updates can be projected into each without leaking other private chat content.
- Prevent runaway agent-to-agent loops, inconsistent commitments and unsupported consensus claims; participant confirmation must be explicit where relevant.

### Q4b — Circle lifetime and A2A collaboration (accepted)

Circle is a scoped collaboration session among independent persistent personal Secretaries, temporary by default, with durable state during collaboration and optional later reuse/resumption. It is not a permanent higher-privilege group AI and does not own participants' private memories. A2A is a plausible means for Secretaries to exchange scoped messages; the coordination mechanism and specific protocol are not yet decided.

Clarification from owner: a Circle may freely **propose** a loan (e.g. asking whether Z would lend Y $100) as a hypothetical option. Such a proposal is not a claim that Z agreed, not a transfer instruction, and not a commitment on anyone's behalf. Prior text overstated this risk; distinction is proposal vs agreement vs actual execution.

### Q4c — Circle information sharing (accepted)

Each Secretary can autonomously share task-relevant conclusions about its user's preferences and constraints to a Circle; routine summaries do not require case-by-case approval. Raw personal records and sensitive details require explicit permission. This is scoped disclosure, not a grant of access to private personal memory. What counts as sensitive, how summaries avoid accidental sensitive disclosure, and audit/revocation semantics remain separate open design branches.

### Current relative question — Q4d (negotiation authority)

Can Circle and participating Secretaries autonomously explore options, make proposals, compare alternatives and recommend an outcome, but only claim agreement when participants have affirmatively agreed (or explicitly delegated decision authority), and never perform consequential external commitments such as purchases, bookings or loans without the applicable participant's authorization? Not decided.
